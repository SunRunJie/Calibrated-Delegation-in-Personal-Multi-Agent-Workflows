# Semi-structured interview guide

**Purpose:** Study 2, task-level delegation and continued use

**Target:** 3-5 workshop participants, 20-30 minutes each

**Preparation:** two copies of the consent form, recording equipment, and this guide. Confirm consent to recording before starting.

Proceed in order from Q1 to Q6. Follow-up prompts are optional. Participants may describe concrete experiences in their own sequence; return to the guide afterward.

## Opening

“Thank you for taking part. I would like to understand your actual experience using the personal AI chief-of-staff system. There are no right answers. Please describe how you use it and how you think about it. With your permission, I will record the interview only for transcription and research analysis. The material will be anonymized, and the recording will be deleted after analysis. You may stop or skip any question. May we begin?”

## Q1. Current use

“Are you still using your agent? What do you mainly use it for?”

- Follow up: How often? What was the most recent use context?
- Record: frequency, typical contexts, and current-use status.

## Q2. Most successful delegation

“Please describe your most successful delegation, starting from how you expressed the request.”

- Follow up: What was the task? How did you communicate it? What was the outcome? What did you check?
- Record: delegated stages, request formulation, verification, and success criteria.

## Q3. Least successful delegation

“Please describe your least successful delegation. What happened?”

- Follow up: What went wrong? How did you respond? Did you delegate that kind of task again?
- Record: failure stage, error, response (withdraw, retry, or reconfigure), and subsequent behavior.

## Q4. Withdrawn tasks

“Have you tried delegating any tasks and later taken them back? Why?”

- Follow up: Did you withdraw an entire task type or only a stage? Did you try again later?
- Record: stage, reason, and level of withdrawal.

## Q5. Effect of memory

“Does the fact that the system remembers you affect how you use it?”

- Follow up: Has it recalled preferences or prior events? Did that increase willingness to delegate? What would be lost if you changed tools?
- Record: perceived memory, personalization, and perceived switching cost.

## Q6. Boundaries and expectations

“What would you like it to do? What would you never want it to do?”

- Follow up: Should it ever stop you proactively? What would you refuse to delegate?
- Record: desired expansion and non-delegable boundaries involving values, privacy, emotion, or consequences.

## Closing

“That covers my questions. Is there an important experience we have not discussed? Thank you. The material will be used anonymously, and your identity will not appear in the research output.”

## Transcription and coding

1. Transcribe within 24 hours when possible, retaining meaningful pauses or laughter.
2. Code a unit as one passage addressing one event or viewpoint.
3. A passage may receive multiple themes.
4. One coder completes the thematic coding; a second person reviews the critical themes of delegation boundaries, withdrawal, and perceived memory. Resolve disagreements through discussion and record the resolution.

| Code | Theme | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Delegation boundary | Workflow stages delegated and differences between stages |
| T2 | Reason for delegation | Capability, time saving, quality, or reduced effort |
| T3 | Withdrawal event | A task or stage delegated and later taken back |
| T4 | Error experience and response | Error visibility, correction, and later behavior |
| T5 | Perceived memory | Recall, personalization, and contextual continuity |
| T6 | Continuance intention | Intention to continue, expand, or recommend use |
| T7 | Boundary expansion | Work made possible that the user previously could not perform |
| T8 | Privacy and risk | Privacy, consequence, or confidentiality concerns |

Evidence strength: 1 = explicit statement, 2 = indirect indication, 3 = mention only.
